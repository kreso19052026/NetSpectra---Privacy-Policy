NetSpectra — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 12 October 2026

NetSpectra (“the app”) is a Wi-Fi, mobile network and Bluetooth analyzer for Android, developed by Kozo Technology. This policy explains what the app stores, what leaves your phone, and the choices you have.

Contact: kozo.technology@gmail.com

In short

  • Everything NetSpectra measures (signal readings, scans, network names, device lists, locations of walk tests and background checks) stays on your phone, in an encrypted database.
  • There is no account and no NetSpectra server. We never receive your measurements.
  • The app is free and shows ads from Google AdMob. AdMob collects some data to serve ads (see below); your measurements, scans and location are never passed to it.

The database is encrypted with a key kept in the Android Keystore. Settings → Background checks → Delete collected data removes the background-check history at any time. Uninstalling the app deletes everything it stored. Android backups of the app are turned off, so this data is not copied to the cloud.

Location and other permissions

Android requires location access to read Wi-Fi scan results, the mobile cell and Bluetooth devices, and to place walk tests on the map. Background location (“Allow all the time”) is used only for the optional background checks, which are off until you turn them on. Location data never leaves your phone. Notification permission is used for security alerts, the weekly report and the running speed test.

What leaves your phone

Only when you use the features below, and only what they need:

  • Speed test (Measurement Lab). Tests run against servers of Measurement Lab (M-Lab), an open internet research platform. M-Lab publishes every test result as open data, including your public IP address. The app asks you before the first test. See M-Lab's privacy policy.
  • Ping, DNS benchmark, traceroute. These send ordinary network probes to the addresses you choose or to public DNS resolvers (e.g. Google, Cloudflare, Quad9).
  • Walk-test map. Map images come from OpenFreeMap; like any web request, the tile server sees your IP address and the map area being viewed.
  • Traceroute map. The free DB-IP Lite location database is downloaded once (and refreshed weekly on Wi-Fi if you allow it); look-ups then happen on the phone. IP Geolocation by DB-IP.

Exporting and sharing

  • Export (Settings → Your data). Saves your results, walk tests and background checks to a file in a place you pick (for example Downloads or a cloud folder you choose). The app writes nothing else and sends nothing; what happens to the file afterwards is up to you. With “Leave out locations and network names” the file contains no positions, Wi-Fi names or walk files.
  • Restore reads only the file you pick.
  • Share a result. The share button on a finished test passes a one-line text summary to the app you choose in Android's share sheet. Nothing is sent unless you tap it.

Advertising and your consent

This app shows ads served by Google AdMob. AdMob may collect your device's advertising ID, IP address (used for approximate location), device and operating-system information, and ad interaction data to serve and measure ads. See how Google uses this information: How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services.

European Economic Area, UK and Switzerland: the app asks for your consent through Google's certified consent management platform. Personalised ads are shown only if you consent; otherwise you may see limited, non-personalised ads. The app works the same either way.

United States: if you live in a US state with a consumer privacy law (such as California), you can opt out of the sale or sharing of your personal information for targeted advertising.

Change your choice at any time: open the app's Settings → Privacy & ads → Privacy options.

Ads never load while a measurement runs, and no measurement, scan or location data is shared with the ad SDK.

Ad-free for 24 hours: if you choose to watch a rewarded video ad to the end, the app shows no ads for the next 24 hours. Only the time the pass ends is stored, encrypted, on your phone.

Children

NetSpectra is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect data from them.

Security research only

The security features are defensive: they look at what your own phone can already see. The app never attacks, disconnects or intercepts other devices.

Changes to this policy
